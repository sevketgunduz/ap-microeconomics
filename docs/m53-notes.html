The headline. Module 53 is well aligned. Topic 3.5 has one essential knowledge statement, and the module delivers it with a table, a marginal table and a figure: CBA-2.D.1 says firms "maximize their profits by comparing marginal revenue and marginal cost", and pp. 537–539 do exactly that, on Jennifer and Jason's tomato farm, by both routes — the profit column and the marginal comparison. Both learning-objective verbs, define and explain, are met "using graphs or data", as the framework asks. What needs adding is a name and a generalisation, not content. The framework calls it the profit-maximizing rule; Krugman calls it the optimal output rule. The first phrase occurs 0 times in all 927 pages; the second occurs 0 times in the CED and 0 times on the released papers, where students meet MR = MC and profit-maximizing quantity (32 hits). And because the module's firm sells at a constant price, it never shows marginal revenue that differs from price. CBA-2.D.1 is stated for firms, not for price takers; Krugman says the rule holds "for any profit-maximizing firm in any market structure" only on p. 584 (Module 58). Entry 14 of the guide supplies both. One AP Review key needs a caution. Tackle the Test MCQ 4 (p. 540) keys "a firm should continue to produce as long as its … economic profit is at least zero". That is the long-run rule. In the short run a firm keeps producing at a loss whenever price covers average variable cost — Topic 3.6, PRD-2.A.1, and Krugman's own Module 59, pp. 592–594.

The essential knowledge statement this topic carries, verbatim:

CBA-2.D.1 (CED p. 66) — "Firms are assumed to produce output to maximize their profits by comparing marginal revenue and marginal cost."

Verdict: well aligned. One statement, delivered completely, with the two kinds of evidence the learning objective names — a table (Tables 53.1 and 53.2) and a graph (Figure 53.1). The gaps in section 3 are a label and a generalisation; neither needs a new lesson.

The topic is short in the framework and central on the exam. Marginal revenue occurs 23 times in this six-page module and 132 times across the eight released papers; profit-maximizing occurs 81 times on the same papers. Every market-structure question in Units 3 and 4 starts here.

Krugman's two objectives (p. 536):

  1. The principle of marginal analysis
  2. How to determine the profit-maximizing level of output using the optimal output rule

Objective 1 reaches back to Topic 1.6 (CBA-2.B, CED p. 40) and Module 51; objective 2 is CBA-2.D.1.

The margin definitions, in Krugman's own words

Principle of marginal analysis (p. 537) — "According to the principle of marginal analysis, every activity should continue until marginal benefit equals marginal cost."
Marginal revenue (p. 537) — "Marginal revenue is the change in total revenue generated by an additional unit of output."
Optimal output rule (p. 537) — "The optimal output rule says that profit is maximized by producing the quantity of output at which the marginal revenue of the last unit produced is equal to its marginal cost."
Marginal cost curve (p. 538) — "The marginal cost curve shows how the cost of producing one more unit depends on the quantity that has already been produced."
Marginal revenue curve (p. 538) — "The marginal revenue curve shows how marginal revenue varies as output varies."

Marginal cost itself is not margin-defined here: Table 53.2 computes it as ΔTC/ΔQ, and Module 55 defines it formally. Treat it as assessable now — CBA-2.D.1 cannot be met without it.

The two routes to the same answer

Krugman finds the profit-maximizing output twice, and the pair is the lesson:

RouteWhereWhat the student doesAnswer
TotalTable 53.1, p. 537Compute TR − TC at every quantity and pick the largest5 bushels, profit $18
MarginalTable 53.2, p. 538Compute MR − MC for each bushel and stop before it turns negative5 bushels — the fifth adds $2, the sixth would lose $2

The marginal route is the one the framework names and the exam tests; the total route is the proof that it works. Teach the total route first and let the marginal route arrive as the shortcut.

5. Corrections to make explicitly in class

"Produce as long as economic profit is at least zero" is the long-run rule

Tackle the Test MCQ 4 (p. 540), keyed (e), and the sentence on p. 539 — "a firm's decision to produce or not, to stay in business or to close down permanently, should be based on economic profit" — both fold two decisions into one. The framework separates them:

PRD-2.A.1 (CED p. 67) — "In the short run, firms decide to operate (i.e., produce positive output) or shut down (i.e., produce zero output) by comparing total revenue to total variable cost or price to average variable cost (AVC)."

A firm with a negative economic profit keeps producing in the short run if its revenue covers its variable cost, because shutting down still leaves it paying its fixed cost. Krugman makes this point himself in Module 59: "whenever price falls between minimum average total cost and minimum average variable cost, the firm is better off producing some output in the short run" (p. 594). Keep the key for MCQ 4, but add "in the long run", and preview the shut-down rule in one sentence. Test question 24 checks it.

The rule is MR = MC, not P = MC

Krugman's firm sells at a constant $18, so MR and P are the same number and Figure 53.1 labels the line MR = P. Students generalise the label. Say now that the price-taker case is a special case: the rule compares marginal revenue with marginal cost for every firm, and price equals marginal revenue only when the firm can sell any quantity at the market price. A student who writes "P = MC" for a monopolist in Unit 4 loses the point. Test questions 20 and 21 check it.

The first marginal cost is not the fixed cost

Table 53.1 starts at a total cost of $14 at zero output. The marginal cost of the first bushel is $30 − $14 = $16, not $30 and not $14; Krugman's MCQ 2 has both as distractors. The $14 is a cost the farm pays whatever it produces, and it has no effect on which quantity maximises profit.

Stop where MR ≥ MC for the last unit — not where MC first exceeds MR

When no quantity makes MR exactly equal MC, the firm produces the last unit whose marginal revenue is at least its marginal cost (p. 537; FRQ 1's rubric). Students who stop at "the first unit where MC > MR" produce one unit too many. Drill the two sentences together: produce a unit if it adds at least as much revenue as cost; stop before the first unit that does not.

Marginal cost crosses marginal revenue twice

The swoosh means a low price line can cut the MC curve on its falling part as well as its rising part. At a price of $14 Krugman's farm has a crossing near 0.7 bushels and another at 4. The first is a profit minimum: every unit after it adds more revenue than cost. Profit is maximised where MC crosses MR from below. Figure 53.1 at $18 never shows this, because the falling part of the curve never reaches $18; the guide's widget at $14 does.

Total revenue is not the goal

Total revenue at a constant price rises with every unit sold, so "sell as much as possible" maximises revenue and not profit. At $18 Jennifer and Jason's revenue is largest at 7 bushels, where profit is $10 against $18 at 5.

7. Suggested allocation — one and a half periods

Krugman places Modules 52–53 before the production and cost modules; the framework places Topics 3.4 and 3.5 after Topics 3.1–3.3. Either order works for this module, because it uses only total and marginal cost. In the framework's order, students arrive already knowing why MC is swoosh-shaped; in Krugman's, the half-period on marginal cost below has to carry the definition.

faster (10 min).

  1. The discrete case: the last unit with MR ≥ MC; FRQ 1 in class (15 min).
  2. MCQ 1–3 as an exit ticket (5 min).

point E (10 min).

is zero — normal profit — and MC crosses MR twice (10 min).
