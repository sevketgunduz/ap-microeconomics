    Spreading the fixed cost

    Crispian's Candies pays $4,800 a month in fixed costs and $1.50 in variable cost for every candy, and sells at $4 (Hoang Question 14.9, p. 211). Change its output and watch the average costs.

    The whole family of cost curves

    Most firms' variable cost rises slowly at first and then fast. Move along the output axis and read every cost at once.

    Illustrative firm: FC = $30, VC = 24Q − 4.5Q² + 0.5Q³, so MC = 24 − 9Q + 1.5Q². It is the same firm as the graph in the Module 53 lab.

    Marginal cost is marginal product upside down

    Take the bakery from the Module 54 lab and pay each baker a daily wage. The cost of each extra loaf is the wage divided by what the baker adds.

    For the teacher: where Hoang needs correcting or adding to
    • Hoang's margin note on p. 189 defines average fixed cost as production costs "that do not change with the level of output". That is total fixed cost. Average fixed cost falls as output rises, as Hoang's own p. 193 shows.
    • No cost curve ever shifts in Hoang. Topic 3.2's statement that cost curves shift with input costs and productivity is added in full; Hoang has the supply-curve version only (costs of factors of production, Chapter 4).
    • Hoang states that MC cuts average cost at its minimum (pp. 193–194) but not that it also cuts average variable cost at its minimum, which the exam's graphs need.
    • Hoang p. 58 says marginal costs "rise with each successive unit produced". That ignores the falling part of MC that follows from the increasing marginal returns Hoang describes on p. 57.
    • Hoang's margin note on p. 194 says long-run average cost equals AFC plus AVC. In the long run there are no fixed costs; that sentence belongs to the short run.
    • Vocabulary: Hoang's AC or SRAC is the exam's ATC; Hoang's SRAVC is AVC.
