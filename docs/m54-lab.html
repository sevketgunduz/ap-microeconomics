    The production function: bakers and loaves

    Hoang's supermarket keeps its checkouts fixed and hires more staff (p. 57). This bakery does the same with its oven. Add bakers one at a time and watch total, marginal and average product.

    Bars show marginal product, orange once diminishing returns have set in; the line is average product. Illustrative bakery: the loaves are invented so that marginal product first rises and then falls, as Hoang describes.

    For the teacher: where Hoang needs correcting or adding to
    • Hoang never measures production. Production function, marginal product and average product each occur 0 times in the book, and total product once, in an unrelated trade example (p. 428). Every calculation in this lab is added for AP.
    • In Hoang the law of diminishing marginal returns is HL only (p. 57). In AP Microeconomics it is core content, and Topic 3.1 adds average product and the marginal–average link.
    • Hoang p. 118 says marginal cost rises because "the marginal factors of production" become "more expensive to employ". The mechanism is productivity, not input prices: with the wage unchanged, marginal cost rises because marginal product falls.
    • Hoang p. 58 says marginal costs "rise with each successive unit produced". That ignores the phase of increasing marginal returns Hoang itself describes on p. 57, when marginal cost falls.
    • Hoang writes marginal returns where the exam writes marginal product, and factor of production where the exam usually writes input.
