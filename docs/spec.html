AP Microeconomics — Course Materials Specification

The source of truth for producing Microeconomics course documents. It is the sibling of ../AP Macroeconomics/macro_course_spec.md and follows the same shape, so the two can be read side by side. Where they differ, the difference is the point.

Anyone — or any agent — working on Micro documents should read this first and follow it rather than improvising a format.

There is no MEA family in Micro. It exists only in Macro, and nothing built against it carries over in either direction.

Exam architecture: 2 hr 10 min, 60 MCQ (70 min, 66.65%), 3 FRQ (60 min, 33.35%) — one long 10-point and two short 5-point questions — four-function calculator. This is identical to Macro, and the AP Economics Skills table (1.A–4.C) is word-for-word the same in both CEDs, so skill practice transfers unchanged.

2. Which Krugman modules serve Micro

Krugman's Economics for AP is sequenced macro-first. Micro gets Sections 1–2 at the front and then jumps to Section 9, 450 pages later. This is the single biggest practical difference from the Macro course, which runs almost straight through.

Module 2 is macroeconomics and carries no AP Microeconomics learning objective. It is the one module in Section 1 to skip.

Section 9 splits across two units. Modules 46–48 are elasticity (Topics 2.3–2.5), 49–50 are surplus and deadweight loss (2.6–2.8), and Module 51, Utility Maximization is where Topics 1.5 and 1.6 actually live — 500 pages after the unit that needs them. Plan for the jump rather than discovering it.

Always look a module up in the index rather than scanning the PDF:

awk -F, '$1=="51"' _extracts/krugman_module_index.csv
# 51,Utility Maximization,...,511,529,552,570   -> pdftotext -f 552 -l 570

3. Document types

Each module produces up to three documents; each unit produces one audit.

3Testtest_module_NN.mdBoth

Structures are identical to the Macro ones and are specified in ../AP Macroeconomics/macro_course_spec.md §4: teaching notes in eight sections with the dependency graph at §7 and the glossary at §8; the student guide as the same spine in the second person with ★ / ○ markers, three examples per entry and two check-yourself questions; the test as a content-validity blueprint over scenario items at Bloom application / analysis / synthesis with all five options explained.

Micro keeps its terms_module_NN.md files, unlike Macro, where the terms reference was withdrawn after measurement showed ~80% duplication of the student guide. The existing Micro ones remain correct for this course. Do not generate new ones — put a new term's definition and examples in the student guide, its CED anchor in notes §1–3, and its prerequisites in notes §7.

4. Non-negotiable criteria

Unchanged from the Macro spec, and they originated here:

  1. Verify by search, not recall. Any claim about what the textbook does or does not contain needs

a full-text search with the count stated. Several findings in this project turn on a term having zero occurrences.

  1. Quote EK statements verbatim from ../_extracts/ced_micro.txt. The exact wording is usually

the finding — MKT-1.A.2 names only land, labor and capital.

  1. Check every number programmatically before it goes in a document.
  2. Cite by EK code with its CED page, never by Enduring-Understanding code across courses.
  3. Distinguish "absent from this module" from "absent from the book." Most gaps are the former.
  4. Every relationship link needs its inverse on the other term.
  5. Say plainly when a module is well aligned. Do not manufacture concerns to fill a section.

5. Micro-specific hazards

Vocabulary runs the opposite way from Macro. Krugman's Module 3 discusses points inside the PPC as unemployment and the frontier as full-employment output, because the same chapter serves both courses. In AP Microeconomics the examinable term is the framework's own: MKT-1.C.2 lists "scarcity, opportunity cost, efficiency, underutilized resources, and economic growth or contraction." Retrain unemployment and recession out of student answers here. Do not carry this correction into the Macro documents — there those words are Topics 2.3 and 2.7 and are correct.

The tiering of Module 1 is almost the inverse of the Macro tiering. Twelve of its seventeen terms are examinable in Micro against five in Macro. Marginal benefit and cost, marginal analysis, the three economic systems, incentives and property rights are all background in Macro and all anchored here — CBA-1/CBA-2 for the first pair, MKT-1.B.2 for the systems, MKT-3.A.1–MKT-3.A.3 for the last two. A Macro document reused unedited will tell a Micro student to stop revising most of Unit 1.

Mixed economy is named by the framework and defined nowhere in the book. MKT-1.B.2 lists command, market and mixed; Krugman defines two of the three. It has to be supplied from outside the text.

Topic 2.9 has no Krugman coverage at all. World price, autarky and terms of trade occur zero times in 927 pages. Use OpenStax or AMSCO, both in Micro Books/, for that topic.

Entrepreneurship is not in the framework. MKT-1.A.2 names land, labor and capital only, while Krugman's own free-response rubric awards a point for entrepreneurship. Teach all four; be explicit that only three are framework terms.

Known Unit 1 coverage, from unit_01_gap_analysis.md: of 24 essential knowledge statements, Krugman Modules 1, 3 and 4 cover 7 fully, 10 partially and 7 not at all.

6. Figures

Generated by a script, never drawn by hand. Micro figures live in figures/make_figures.py, built on the Fig class.

  • Stdlib-only SVG. No matplotlib on this machine.
  • Escape <, > and & in all text — Fig.esc() does it; a raw < silently breaks the file.
  • Label both axes and every curve. Skill 4.A is graded on it.
  • Captions do not wrap — one Fig.caption() call per line, under ~80 characters.
  • Validate every file as XML after generating.

Expected per unit: PPC shapes and shifts (1) · supply and demand with shifts, price controls, surplus and deadweight loss (2) · the full cost-curve family and perfect competition in short and long run (3) · monopoly, price discrimination, monopolistic competition, payoff matrices (4) · factor demand and monopsony (5) · externalities, public goods and Lorenz curves (6).

The supply-and-demand diagram recurs in Units 2, 3, 4 and 6. Build one parameterised generator for it rather than a dozen near-duplicates.

8. Order of work

  1. Modules 1 and 3 are done as student guides, derived from the Macro editions.
  2. Their teaching notes and tests still need Micro editions — the Macro versions cite MOD and MEA

codes throughout and cannot be reused.

  1. Then Module 4 (comparative advantage, Topic 1.4) and Module 51 to close Unit 1.
  2. Then Unit 2, the largest unit: Modules 5–9, then 46–50, then Topic 2.9 from OpenStax.
  3. Per module: teaching notes → student guide → test. Per unit: the gap analysis once its modules are

done.

  1. Publishing a document to the site means changing one build/manifest.micro.json entry from

"kind": "planned" to a real kind. Rebuild with python -X utf8 build/build.py --course=micro.
