The headline. Module 55 is well aligned on everything it teaches, and one statement is missing. Topic 3.2 has five essential knowledge statements (PRD-1.A.4–A.8, CED p. 63). The module delivers four of them on two worked firms: fixed, variable and total cost; marginal cost and the three average costs; why diminishing returns make marginal cost rise; and why specialization makes it fall first — with the full family of curves, the U-shape, and the rule that marginal cost crosses average total cost at its minimum. PRD-1.A.8 is not in the book. "Cost curves can shift in response to changes in input costs and productivity." Shift occurs 0 times in pp. 548–558, productivity 0 times and input cost 0 times, and no firm's cost curve shifts anywhere in pp. 540–793 (the only "shifted upward" curve in that span is the marginal social cost curve of Module 75, p. 739). The book's one brush with the idea is an exercise: the Section 10 review asks students to evaluate "an increase in fixed cost increases the minimum-cost output" (p. 581, problem 16b–c) without having taught it. The guide's entries 14, 15 and 16 supply all three shifts, on Krugman's own salsa numbers. Two graph facts the exam uses are not stated. Marginal cost also crosses average variable cost at its minimum — minimum average variable cost is 0 times in the module, first on p. 593 (Module 59) — and average fixed cost is the vertical gap between ATC and AVC, which the CED's sample question 9 turns on (p. 130, key B). Entries 11 and 13 supply them.

The margin definitions, in Krugman's own words

Fixed cost (p. 548) — "A fixed cost is a cost that does not depend on the quantity of output produced. It is the cost of the fixed input."
Variable cost (p. 548) — "A variable cost is a cost that depends on the quantity of output produced. It is the cost of the variable input."
Total cost (p. 548) — "The total cost of producing a given quantity of output is the sum of the fixed cost and the variable cost of producing that quantity of output."
Total cost curve (p. 549) — "The total cost curve shows how total cost depends on the quantity of output."
Average total cost (p. 552) — "Average total cost, often referred to simply as average cost, is total cost divided by quantity of output produced."
U-shaped average total cost curve (p. 553) — "A U-shaped average total cost curve falls at low levels of output and then rises at higher levels."
Average fixed cost (p. 553) — "Average fixed cost is the fixed cost per unit of output."
Average variable cost (p. 553) — "Average variable cost is the variable cost per unit of output."
Minimum-cost output (p. 555) — "The minimum-cost output is the quantity of output at which average total cost is lowest—it corresponds to the bottom of the U-shaped average total cost curve."

Three points about the wording. The framework writes total fixed cost and total variable cost where Krugman writes fixed cost and variable cost; the first pair is 0 times in the book. Krugman allows average cost for average total cost (p. 552); the exam almost never does (average cost 8 times on the papers, average total cost 109), so insist on the full name. And minimum-cost output is Krugman's term only — teach it, but have students write "the quantity at which average total cost is lowest" on the exam.

5. Corrections to make explicitly in class

Fixed does not mean "can never change"

Fixed cost does not change with output. It can change for other reasons — the rent goes up, a licence fee is imposed — and that is a shift of the cost curves (entry 15). Krugman's Section 10 review asks exactly this (p. 581, 16b–c) after a module that never discusses it. Teach the two sentences together: fixed cost is the same at every output; a change in fixed cost moves ATC and AFC, and nothing else.

Fixed cost and sunk cost — what the exam needs

The framework writes "fixed costs (sunk costs)" (CBA-2.B.2, CED p. 40), and what it tests with the pair is one point: neither changes the best quantity to produce, because neither changes marginal cost. Krugman separates the words in Module 56 (p. 563): a fixed cost is paid whatever the output, a sunk cost cannot be recovered whatever the firm does. Teach the shared point now and the distinction with Module 56; do not mark a student down for using the framework's own parenthesis.

Marginal cost is unaffected by fixed cost

Because ΔTC = ΔVC, the marginal cost of a unit is the same whether computed from total cost or variable cost (Krugman's MCQ 2, statement II). It follows that a change in fixed cost cannot move the marginal cost curve — the Section 10 review's 16c ("An increase in fixed cost increases marginal cost") is false, and the exam's lump-sum-tax questions test the same point.

Average variable cost is U-shaped on the exam

Selena's AVC rises from the first case, and Krugman's text says AVC "also slopes upward" (p. 555). The exam's standard graph — and Krugman's own Figure 55.6 — has a U-shaped AVC with marginal cost passing through its minimum, to the left of ATC's minimum. Teach the salsa numbers for calculation and the swoosh for drawing.

Marginal cost crosses both averages at their minimums, from below

The module says MC crosses ATC "from below, crossing it at its lowest point" (p. 555). Say it for AVC too, and add that AFC is never crossed: it falls at every output. A graph in which MC crosses ATC to the right of its minimum, or crosses AVC anywhere but at its bottom, loses the graphing point.

The gap between ATC and AVC narrows

AFC = ATC − AVC is the vertical distance between the two curves, and since AFC falls at every output the two curves converge as output rises — they never meet. Students who draw ATC and AVC parallel, or crossing, have forgotten that the gap is the falling AFC.

Marginal cost is plotted between quantities

Like marginal product in Module 54, the marginal cost of going from 2 to 3 cases is plotted at 2.5 (p. 551). Krugman's FRQ 1 rubric (p. 558) notes the consequence: a smooth MC drawn through those points crosses ATC at its minimum only approximately; as a step function it crosses exactly.
