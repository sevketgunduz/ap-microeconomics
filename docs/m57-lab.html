    Market power: a lower price to sell more, and a price above marginal cost

    Compare a price taker with a firm that has market power. Fewer close substitutes make the firm's demand steeper. Watch the gap between price and marginal cost.

    Illustrative firm: MC = 5 + 3Q. Price taker: market price $20. Firm with market power: demand P = 50 − bQ, so MR = 50 − 2bQ.

    Barriers to entry and the number of firms

    Economic profit attracts new firms unless something keeps them out. Change the start-up cost a firm must cover, or add a legal limit, and see how many firms the market ends up with.

    Illustrative market: demand P = 100 − Q, marginal cost $20 for every firm. Each firm's profit with n rivals in the market is computed from the model, and firms keep entering while one more can still cover its fixed cost.

    For the teacher: where Hoang needs correcting or adding to
    • Hoang p. 183 says monopolists "enjoy price inelastic demand" and earn more revenue by charging higher prices. A profit-maximizing monopolist produces on the elastic part of its demand curve, where marginal revenue is positive; the CED's sample question 11 lists the inelastic portion as a wrong answer.
    • Monopsony occurs 0 times in Hoang. The CED names it as the fourth imperfectly competitive structure (Topic 4.1), in factor markets; it is added here and taught in Unit 5.
    • Hoang's case study lists Coca-Cola, Google, PayPal and others as monopolies (p. 183). These are dominant firms with market power, mostly in oligopolies; keep "monopoly" for a single seller, as Hoang's own Top tip advises (p. 182).
    • Concentration ratio and Herfindahl-Hirschman Index occur 0 times in the CED; the lab's section on them is background.
    • Hoang's perfect competition adds perfect information (p. 180). The CED's statements name many firms' lack of market power, no barriers to entry, a constant market price and efficiency; information is not required.
    • In Hoang the whole chapter is HL only. In AP Microeconomics it opens Unit 4, the second-heaviest unit on the exam.
