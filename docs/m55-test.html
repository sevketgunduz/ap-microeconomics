Sixteen numeric items, because Topic 3.2 is a table of costs and a graph drawn from it. The synthesis items sit where a student must judge a claim: that ATC and AVC move apart, that a rising marginal cost means a rising average, that both averages bottom out at the same output, and that a higher fixed cost raises marginal cost.

1. What is Harbor Bike Repair's total fixed cost?

  • (A) $0
  • (B) $150
  • (C) $120
  • (D) $30
  • (E) $20

2. A bakery pays monthly rent on its shop, buys flour every morning, pays its bakers by the hour, pays an insurance premium that is the same every month, and pays for the gas its ovens burn, which rises with the number of loaves baked.

Which of these are variable costs?

  • (A) The flour, the bakers' hourly wages and the gas.
  • (B) The rent and the insurance.
  • (C) All five, because every cost is paid each month.
  • (D) The flour only.
  • (E) The bakers' hourly wages and the rent.

3. A print shop has a total fixed cost of $80 a day. Its total variable cost of printing 10 posters is $150.

What is its total cost of printing 10 posters, and where does its total cost curve meet the vertical axis?

  • (A) $150; at zero.
  • (B) $230; at zero.
  • (C) $70; at $80.
  • (D) $23; at $8.
  • (E) $230; at $80.

5. Two students calculate the marginal cost of Harbor's third bicycle. The first uses total variable cost and gets $72 − $48 = $24. The second uses total cost and gets $192 − $168 = $24.

Which evaluation is correct?

  • (A) Both are correct: fixed cost is the same at both outputs, so the change in total cost equals the change in variable cost.
  • (B) Only the second is correct, because marginal cost must be calculated from total cost.
  • (C) Only the first is correct, because fixed cost has to be removed before taking the difference.
  • (D) Neither is correct: marginal cost is total cost divided by output, $192 ÷ 3 = $64.
  • (E) Neither is correct: marginal cost must include a share of the rent, $120 ÷ 3 = $40.

6. A repair workshop pays each mechanic $96 a day, whatever the number of mechanics. The third mechanic hired adds 12 repairs a day; the fourth adds 8.

What is the marginal cost of a repair when the fourth mechanic is hired?

7. For a firm where labor is the only variable input, which of the following happens when diminishing returns set in?

8. Harbor's second bicycle costs $18 to service, less than the first bicycle's $30.

Which is the most likely explanation?

  • (C) The rent fell when the second bicycle arrived.
  • (D) Economies of scale, because the shop is larger.
  • (E) With two mechanics the tasks can be divided, so the second mechanic adds more than the first: specialization.

14. A student says: "As Harbor services more bicycles, its ATC and AVC curves move further apart, because more output means more fixed cost to cover."

Which evaluation is correct?

  • (A) Correct, because total fixed cost rises with output.
  • (B) Incorrect: the gap between the curves is average fixed cost, which falls at every output — from $60 at 2 bicycles to $20 at 6 — so the curves draw together.
  • (C) Incorrect, because the gap between the curves is marginal cost, which rises.
  • (D) Correct, because average variable cost rises faster than average total cost.
  • (E) Incorrect, because the two curves cross at the minimum of average total cost.

17. A student says: "Whenever marginal cost is rising, average total cost must be rising too."

Which evaluation is correct?

  • (A) Correct, because marginal cost is the slope of the average total cost curve.
  • (B) Correct: at Harbor, marginal cost rises from the third bicycle on, and average total cost rises with it.
  • (C) Incorrect: average total cost rises only when marginal cost is above it. At Harbor, marginal cost rises for the third and fourth bicycles while average total cost falls from $84 to $64 to $56.
  • (D) Incorrect, because marginal cost never rises in the short run.
  • (E) Incorrect, because average total cost rises only when average fixed cost rises.

18. At what output is Harbor's average variable cost lowest?

  • (A) At 1 bicycle, where marginal cost and average variable cost are both $30.
  • (B) At 2 and 3 bicycles, $24 — the third bicycle's marginal cost equals average variable cost there.
  • (C) At 5 and 6 bicycles, where marginal cost equals average total cost at $52.
  • (D) At 2 bicycles only, because marginal cost is lowest there, $18.
  • (E) Nowhere: average variable cost rises at every output.

19. A student says: "Marginal cost crosses average variable cost and average total cost at the same output, because both are averages of the same firm's costs."

Which evaluation is correct?

  • (A) Correct, because both curves are U-shaped.
  • (B) Correct, because average fixed cost is constant.
  • (C) Incorrect, because marginal cost never crosses average variable cost.
  • (D) Incorrect, because the bottom of average total cost comes at a smaller output than the bottom of average variable cost.
  • (E) Incorrect: at the bottom of average variable cost, average fixed cost is still falling, so average total cost is still falling. At Harbor, AVC is lowest at 2 and 3 bicycles and ATC at 5 and 6.

23. A student says: "A lump-sum tax on Harbor increases its marginal cost, because its total cost is higher at every output."

Which evaluation is correct?

  • (A) Correct, because every cost the firm pays enters marginal cost.
  • (B) Correct, because average total cost rises.
  • (C) Incorrect, because a lump-sum tax lowers marginal cost.
  • (D) Incorrect, because a lump-sum tax is not part of total cost.

Solutions — every option explained

Harbor's costs, from the table: marginal cost 30, 18, 24, 32, 36 and 52 dollars for the first to the sixth bicycle; total variable cost 30, 48, 72, 104, 140 and 192; average variable cost 30, 24, 24, 26, 28 and 32; average fixed cost 120, 60, 40, 30, 24 and 20; average total cost 150, 84, 64, 56, 52 and 52.

Question 1 — Fixed cost · Application

Answer: (C)

At zero output variable cost is zero, so the whole of the $120 total cost at 0 bicycles is fixed cost — the workshop rent, paid whatever the output.

  • (A) ✗ Total cost is $120 at zero output; a firm with fixed costs has cost even when it produces nothing.
  • (B) ✗ $150 is total cost at 1 bicycle, fixed plus variable.
  • (C) ✓ Correct.
  • (D) ✗ $30 is the variable cost, and the marginal cost, of the first bicycle.
  • (E) ✗ $120 ÷ 6 = $20 is average fixed cost at 6 bicycles.

Question 2 — Variable cost · Analysis

Answer: (A)

A variable cost changes with output. Flour, hours of baking and oven gas all rise with the number of loaves; the rent and the insurance are the same whatever is baked.

  • (A) ✓ Correct.
  • (B) ✗ Reversed: these are the fixed costs.
  • (C) ✗ How often a cost is paid does not make it variable; whether it changes with output does.
  • (D) ✗ The hourly wages and the gas also rise with output.
  • (E) ✗ The rent does not change with the number of loaves.

Question 3 — Total cost · Application

Answer: (E)

Total cost is fixed plus variable cost: $80 + $150 = $230. At zero output variable cost is zero, so the total cost curve starts at the fixed cost, $80.

  • (A) ✗ $150 is variable cost only, and the curve starts at the fixed cost, not at zero.
  • (B) ✗ The total is right, but the curve starts at the fixed cost.
  • (C) ✗ $150 − $80 subtracts instead of adding.
  • (D) ✗ $230 ÷ 10 = $23 is average total cost, and $80 ÷ 10 = $8 is average fixed cost.
  • (E) ✓ Correct.

Question 4 — Marginal cost · Application

Answer: (B)

Marginal cost is the change in total cost: $224 − $192 = $32.

  • (A) ✗ $224 is total cost at 4 bicycles.
  • (B) ✓ Correct.
  • (C) ✗ $224 ÷ 4 = $56 is average total cost.
  • (D) ✗ $104 ÷ 4 = $26 is average variable cost.
  • (E) ✗ $224 − $120 = $104 is total variable cost at 4 bicycles.

Question 5 — Marginal cost · Analysis

Answer: (A)

Fixed cost is $120 at both outputs, so it cancels from the difference: $192 − $168 = ($120 + $72) − ($120 + $48) = $72 − $48 = $24. Either total gives the same marginal cost.

  • (A) ✓ Correct.
  • (B) ✗ The change in variable cost is the same number, because fixed cost does not change.
  • (C) ✗ Removing fixed cost first is allowed but not required; both routes give $24.
  • (D) ✗ $192 ÷ 3 = $64 is average total cost, not the cost of the third bicycle.
  • (E) ✗ Rent is paid whatever the output, so none of it is the cost of one more bicycle.

Question 6 — Diminishing returns raise marginal cost · Application

Answer: (C)

The fourth mechanic costs $96 and adds 8 repairs, so each added repair costs $96 ÷ 8 = $12.

  • (A) ✗ $96 ÷ 12 = $8 is the marginal cost with the third mechanic.
  • (B) ✗ $96 × 8 multiplies instead of dividing.
  • (C) ✓ Correct.
  • (D) ✗ 12 − 8 = 4 is the fall in marginal product, not a cost.
  • (E) ✗ $96 is the cost of a mechanic, not of a repair.

Question 7 — Diminishing returns raise marginal cost · Analysis

Answer: (D)

Diminishing returns set in where the marginal product of labor begins to fall. Marginal cost is the wage divided by the marginal product, so at that point marginal cost begins to rise.

  • (A) ✗ Average variable cost rises only once marginal cost has risen above it, which comes later.
  • (B) ✗ Average product falls only once marginal product has fallen below it, which comes later.
  • (C) ✗ Total product keeps rising while marginal product is positive.
  • (D) ✓ Correct.
  • (E) ✗ Average total cost rises only once marginal cost is above it, later still.

Question 8 — Specialization lowers marginal cost · Analysis

Answer: (E)

A falling marginal cost at low output means a rising marginal product: with two mechanics one can strip and one can fit, and the second adds more than the first.

  • (A) ✗ Diminishing marginal returns raise marginal cost; here it fell.
  • (B) ✗ Spreading fixed cost lowers average fixed cost, not the cost of one more bicycle.
  • (C) ✗ Nothing in the table says the rent changed; it is $120 at every output.
  • (D) ✗ Economies of scale are a long-run idea, with every input varied; the workshop is fixed.
  • (E) ✓ Correct.

Question 9 — Average total cost · Application

Answer: (B)

Average total cost is total cost over output: $192 ÷ 3 = $64.

  • (A) ✗ $24 is the third bicycle's marginal cost, and also the average variable cost at 3.
  • (B) ✓ Correct.
  • (C) ✗ $120 ÷ 3 = $40 is average fixed cost.
  • (D) ✗ $192 − $120 = $72 is total variable cost.
  • (E) ✗ $192 is total cost, not divided by output.

Question 10 — Average fixed cost · Application

Answer: (D)

Average fixed cost is fixed cost over output: $120 ÷ 4 = $30.

  • (A) ✗ $224 ÷ 4 = $56 is average total cost.
  • (B) ✗ $104 ÷ 4 = $26 is average variable cost.
  • (C) ✗ $32 is the fourth bicycle's marginal cost.
  • (D) ✓ Correct.
  • (E) ✗ $120 is total fixed cost, not divided by output.

Question 11 — Average variable cost · Application

Answer: (A)

Total variable cost at 5 bicycles is $260 − $120 = $140, so average variable cost is $140 ÷ 5 = $28.

  • (A) ✓ Correct.
  • (B) ✗ $260 ÷ 5 = $52 is average total cost.
  • (C) ✗ $36 is the fifth bicycle's marginal cost.
  • (D) ✗ $120 ÷ 5 = $24 is average fixed cost.
  • (E) ✗ $140 is total variable cost, not divided by output.

Question 12 — Why average total cost is U-shaped · Analysis

Answer: (C)

From 2 to 3 bicycles average fixed cost falls from $60 to $40, by $20, while average variable cost stays at $24. So average total cost falls by the full $20, from $84 to $64: the spreading effect outweighs the diminishing returns effect.

  • (A) ✗ A rising marginal cost cannot by itself lower the average; what falls here is average fixed cost.
  • (B) ✗ Average variable cost is $24 at both 2 and 3 bicycles.
  • (C) ✓ Correct.
  • (D) ✗ When the diminishing returns effect outweighs, average total cost rises.
  • (E) ✗ Total fixed cost is $120 at every output; it is the fixed cost per bicycle that falls.

Question 13 — Average fixed cost is the gap between the curves · Application

Answer: (E)

The gap between ATC and AVC is average fixed cost: $52 − $28 = $24, which is $120 ÷ 5.

  • (A) ✗ $36 is the fifth bicycle's marginal cost.
  • (B) ✗ $120 is total fixed cost; the gap is per bicycle.
  • (C) ✗ $28 is the height of the AVC curve, not the gap above it.
  • (D) ✗ $52 is the height of the ATC curve.
  • (E) ✓ Correct.

Question 14 — Average fixed cost is the gap between the curves · Synthesis

Answer: (B)

The vertical gap between ATC and AVC is average fixed cost. Total fixed cost is the same at every output, so the gap falls — $60, $40, $30, $24, $20 from 2 to 6 bicycles — and the curves draw together.

  • (A) ✗ Total fixed cost is $120 at every output.
  • (B) ✓ Correct.
  • (C) ✗ Right conclusion, wrong reason: the gap is average fixed cost, not marginal cost.
  • (D) ✗ ATC is AVC plus a falling AFC, so ATC rises more slowly than AVC, never faster.
  • (E) ✗ Right conclusion, wrong reason: ATC and AVC never cross; marginal cost crosses each.

Question 15 — Minimum-cost output · Analysis

Answer: (D)

A new unit that costs exactly the average leaves the average unchanged: (5 × $52 + $52) ÷ 6 = $312 ÷ 6 = $52. Average total cost is at its lowest here, at both 5 and 6 bicycles.

  • (A) ✗ The average rises only if the new unit costs more than the average, not if marginal cost is rising.
  • (B) ✗ Spreading the rent lowers average fixed cost, but average variable cost rises by as much.
  • (C) ✗ Where marginal cost meets average total cost, the average is at its lowest, not its highest.
  • (D) ✓ Correct.
  • (E) ✗ The average of 5 bicycles and the sixth's marginal cost are enough to find the new average.

Question 16 — Minimum-cost output · Application

Answer: (A)

Total cost of 8 units is 8 × $40 = $320. The ninth adds $22, so average total cost is ($320 + $22) ÷ 9 = $342 ÷ 9 = $38. Marginal cost is below the average, so the average falls.

  • (A) ✓ Correct.
  • (B) ✗ ($40 + $22) ÷ 2 = $31 averages the two numbers as if each were one unit.
  • (C) ✗ The average moves toward the marginal cost, not all the way to it.
  • (D) ✗ The average stays the same only if the new unit costs exactly $40.
  • (E) ✗ $40 + $22 adds the marginal cost to the average.

Question 17 — Minimum-cost output · Synthesis

Answer: (C)

What moves the average is whether marginal cost is above or below it. At Harbor marginal cost rises from $18 to $24 to $32 for the second to fourth bicycles, but each is below the average before it, so average total cost keeps falling.

  • (A) ✗ Marginal cost is the slope of the total cost curve, not of the average total cost curve.
  • (B) ✗ Average total cost falls until 5 bicycles, while marginal cost has been rising since the third.
  • (C) ✓ Correct.
  • (D) ✗ Right conclusion, wrong reason: marginal cost rises in the short run once diminishing returns set in.
  • (E) ✗ Right conclusion, wrong reason: average fixed cost falls at every output, yet average total cost does rise.

Question 18 — Marginal cost crosses average variable cost at its minimum · Analysis

Answer: (B)

Average variable cost is 30, 24, 24, 26, 28 and 32. It is lowest, $24, at 2 and 3 bicycles, and the third bicycle's marginal cost, $24, equals the average there: that is where marginal cost crosses AVC.

  • (A) ✗ At one unit marginal cost always equals average variable cost; AVC falls after it.
  • (B) ✓ Correct.
  • (C) ✗ That is the bottom of average total cost, not of average variable cost.
  • (D) ✗ Average variable cost is still falling to its lowest when marginal cost is lowest; the bottom of AVC is where marginal cost reaches it.
  • (E) ✗ Average variable cost falls from $30 to $24 before it rises.

Question 19 — Marginal cost crosses average variable cost at its minimum · Synthesis

Answer: (E)

ATC is AVC plus AFC. At the bottom of AVC, AFC is still falling, so ATC is still falling and reaches its own bottom only at a larger output. At Harbor, AVC is lowest at 2 and 3 bicycles and ATC at 5 and 6.

  • (A) ✗ Both are U-shaped, but their bottoms are at different outputs.
  • (B) ✗ Average fixed cost falls at every output; it is total fixed cost that is constant.
  • (C) ✗ Right conclusion, wrong reason: marginal cost crosses average variable cost at its minimum.
  • (D) ✗ Right conclusion, wrong reason: the order is reversed — the bottom of AVC comes first.
  • (E) ✓ Correct.

Question 20 — A change in an input price shifts the cost curves · Analysis

Answer: (D)

Labor is the variable input, so a higher wage raises variable cost at every output: marginal, average variable and average total cost all shift up. The rent is unchanged, so average fixed cost does not move.

  • (A) ✗ That is the effect of a higher rent, a fixed cost.
  • (B) ✗ Average variable and average total cost rise as well, at every output.
  • (C) ✗ Average fixed cost depends only on the rent and the output.
  • (D) ✓ Correct.
  • (E) ✗ The wage bill rises with the number of mechanics, so it is a variable cost.

Question 21 — A change in an input price shifts the cost curves · Application

Answer: (B)

Every bicycle now costs $4 more, so each marginal cost rises by $4: the third is $24 + $4 = $28. Average variable cost, and so average total cost, also rise by $4 at every output: $64 + $4 = $68.

  • (A) ✗ The extra $4 is paid on every bicycle, including the third, so marginal cost rises too.
  • (B) ✓ Correct.
  • (C) ✗ Average total cost includes the $4 on each of the three bicycles.
  • (D) ✗ Adds 3 × $4 = $12 to both; the rise is $4 per bicycle, so the third bicycle and the average each rise by $4.
  • (E) ✗ $64 + $4 ÷ 3 treats the $4 as a fixed cost spread over 3 bicycles.

Question 22 — A change in fixed cost · Application

Answer: (A)

Total cost at 5 bicycles rises by $60, to $320, so average total cost is $320 ÷ 5 = $64. Marginal cost is the change in total cost between 4 and 5 bicycles, and both totals rose by $60, so the fifth bicycle still costs $260 − $224 = $36.

  • (A) ✓ Correct.
  • (B) ✗ Adds the $12 rise in average fixed cost to marginal cost, which fixed cost does not enter.
  • (C) ✗ $52 + $60 adds the whole rise in rent to the cost of each bicycle; it is spread over 5.
  • (D) ✗ Average total cost rises by $60 ÷ 5 = $12.
  • (E) ✗ Adds the whole $60 to marginal cost.

Question 23 — A change in fixed cost · Synthesis

Answer: (E)

A lump-sum tax is a fixed cost. It raises total cost by the same amount at every output, so the difference between neighbouring totals — marginal cost — is unchanged. Average fixed and average total cost rise.

  • (A) ✗ Only costs that change with output enter marginal cost.
  • (B) ✗ Average total cost does rise, but that says nothing about marginal cost.
  • (C) ✗ Right conclusion, wrong reason: a lump-sum tax leaves marginal cost unchanged; it does not lower it.
  • (D) ✗ Right conclusion, wrong reason: the tax is part of total cost, as a fixed cost.
  • (E) ✓ Correct.

Question 24 — A change in productivity shifts the cost curves · Analysis

Answer: (C)

Each mechanic now services more bicycles for the same wage: marginal product rises, so marginal cost — the wage divided by marginal product — falls, and so do average variable and average total cost. The rent is unchanged, so average fixed cost does not move.

  • (A) ✗ Reversed: higher productivity lowers costs.
  • (B) ✗ Fixed cost did not change; it is variable cost per bicycle that falls.
  • (C) ✓ Correct.
  • (D) ✗ Average fixed cost depends only on the rent and the output.
  • (E) ✗ The update changes how many bicycles each paid hour produces, which changes cost even though the update is free.

Question 25 — A change in productivity shifts the cost curves · Application

Answer: (D)

Marginal cost is the wage divided by marginal product: $96 ÷ 12 = $8 before and $96 ÷ 16 = $6 after.

  • (A) ✗ Reversed: a higher marginal product lowers marginal cost.
  • (B) ✗ The wage is unchanged, but each bicycle now takes less of it.
  • (C) ✗ The cost per bicycle falls when each day's work produces more bicycles.
  • (D) ✓ Correct.
  • (E) ✗ $96 is the cost of the mechanic, not of a bicycle.

Notes for the teacher

Content validity. All 16 concept entries appear. The totals and marginal cost (entries 1 to 4) carry five items; why marginal cost has its shape (entries 5 and 6) three; the three averages and the U (entries 7 to 10) four; the gap, the minimum-cost output and the crossing of AVC (entries 11 to 13) seven; and the three shifts (entries 14 to 16) six. The five flagged entries — 11, 13, 14, 15 and 16 — carry ten items between them, because the book does not teach them and the shifts are a standard free-response part.

Bloom levels. Application 12 · Analysis 9 · Synthesis 4, a ratio of 1 : 0.75 : 0.33 against the standard's 1 : 3 : 1.75. The paper is more numerical than the standard because Topic 3.2 is a cost table and the graph drawn from it, and the released papers test it from both.

Question 7 is the CED's sample question 7, word for word (p. 129, key D). Module 54's test teaches its trap from the production side; here it is asked as written, from the cost side, which is the side its key is on.

Questions 15, 17 and 19 are the ones to discuss aloud. Question 15 is the boundary case: a unit that costs exactly the average leaves it unchanged, which is why the bottom of a U is where marginal cost meets it. Question 17 separates a rising marginal cost from a rising average. Question 19 is the order of the two bottoms, which the book never states.

Questions 21 to 23 are the shifts the book never makes. A per-unit change in an input's cost raises marginal cost and both averages by the same amount; a change in rent raises average total cost and leaves marginal cost alone. Question 23 is Krugman's own Section 10 review problem 16c (p. 581), "An increase in fixed cost increases marginal cost", restated with the exam's lump-sum tax.

Distractor design. Every wrong option encodes a documented error. The recurring ones: a total read as a per-unit figure or the reverse (1E, 3D, 4A, 4E, 9E, 10E, 11E, 13B); one average read as another (4C, 4D, 9C, 10A, 10B, 11B, 11D, 13C, 13D); marginal cost read as an average or the reverse (1D, 5D, 9A, 10C, 11C, 13A); fixed cost thought to enter marginal cost (5E, 22B, 22E, 23A); fixed and variable costs confused (2B, 2C, 2E, 20A, 20E); the curve's intercept misplaced (1A, 3A, 3B); the wage and the marginal product confused (6A, 6B, 6D, 6E, 25A, 25B, 25C, 25E); diminishing returns and specialization swapped, or scale offered instead (8A, 8D); spreading and diminishing returns effects confused (12A, 12B, 12D, 12E); the marginal–average rule misapplied (15A, 15C, 16B, 16C, 16D, 16E, 17A, 17B, 18D); the two bottoms placed together or reversed (18C, 19A, 19D); and a shift read as the wrong set of curves (20B, 20C, 21A, 21C, 21D, 21E, 22C, 22D, 24A, 24B, 24D, 24E).

Eight distractors are right-conclusion-wrong-reason, flagged in the solutions: 14C, 14E, 17D, 17E, 19C, 19D, 23C and 23D each reach the correct verdict, "incorrect", for a reason that is false.
