This paper assumes Module 52. Every profit in it is an economic profit: total cost includes implicit costs, as Krugman assumes on p. 539. Questions 23 and 24 go back to that distinction.
Questions 20 and 21 test what the module never shows. CBA-2.D.1 says firms maximize profit "by comparing marginal revenue and marginal cost" — it is stated for every firm. Module 53's farm sells at a constant price, so its marginal revenue never differs from price.
Vocabulary. The stems use the exam's words — profit-maximizing, marginal revenue equals marginal cost — and never Krugman's optimal output rule, which occurs 0 times in the CED and 0 times on the released papers.

  • (C) Total revenue minus all costs, implicit costs included.
  • (D) Total revenue minus the costs the bakery actually pays out in money.
  • (E) The difference between price and marginal cost on each cake.

Solutions — every option explained

The bakery's marginal costs, from the total-cost table, are $12, $8, $10, $14, $18, $24, $30 and $38 for the first to the eighth cake.

Question 1 — Profit maximization · Analysis

Answer: (C)

Firms are assumed to maximize economic profit: total revenue minus every cost, including the implicit cost of the owner's time and capital.

  • (A) ✗ Selling more is not the goal; extra cakes can cost more than they bring in.
  • (B) ✗ Revenue keeps rising after profit has started to fall.
  • (C) ✓ Correct.
  • (D) ✗ That is accounting profit, which leaves out implicit costs.
  • (E) ✗ The gap on a single cake is not the total; the firm adds units while that gap is not negative.

Question 2 — Profit is total revenue minus total cost · Application

Answer: (A)

Profit at $21 is −$30, −$21, −$8, $3, $10, $13, $10, $1 and −$16 for 0 to 8 cakes. The largest is $21 × 5 − $92 = $13, at 5 cakes.

  • (A) ✓ Correct.
  • (B) ✗ $168 is total revenue at 8 cakes; profit there is −$16.
  • (C) ✗ $10 at 4 cakes is less than $13 at 5.
  • (D) ✗ The sixth cake costs $24 to bake and brings in $21, so profit falls to $10.
  • (E) ✗ $105 is total revenue at 5 cakes, not profit.

Question 3 — Profit is total revenue minus total cost · Synthesis

Answer: (E)

At 8 cakes revenue is $168 but cost is $184, so profit is $168 − $184 = −$16. At 5 cakes it is $13. Revenue is largest where every cake is sold, but the last three cakes each cost more to bake than the $21 they bring in.

  • (A) ✗ True that revenue rises, but cost rises faster after the fifth cake.
  • (B) ✗ The manager's premise; profit depends on cost as well.
  • (C) ✗ Total cost is lowest at zero output, where profit is −$30.
  • (D) ✗ The lowest marginal cost is not where profit peaks; profit at 2 cakes is −$8.
  • (E) ✓ Correct.

Question 4 — The principle of marginal analysis · Analysis

Answer: (B)

Keep going while marginal benefit is at least marginal cost. The third hour is worth $12 against a $10 cost; the fourth is worth $8. Net benefit is $50 − $20 = $30 at 2 hours, $62 − $30 = $32 at 3, and $70 − $40 = $30 at 4.

  • (A) ✗ Total benefit is largest at 4 hours, but the fourth hour costs more than it is worth.
  • (B) ✓ Correct.
  • (C) ✗ Stops while the third hour is still worth more than it costs.
  • (D) ✗ The largest marginal benefit is not the stopping point.
  • (E) ✗ An hour is worth taking whenever its benefit is at least its cost.

Question 5 — Marginal revenue · Application

Answer: (D)

Total revenue rises from $42 to $63 with the third cake: marginal revenue is $63 − $42 = $21, the price.

  • (A) ✗ $63 is total revenue at 3 cakes.
  • (B) ✗ $10 is the third cake's marginal cost.
  • (C) ✗ $3 is the profit at 3 cakes.
  • (D) ✓ Correct.
  • (E) ✗ $11 is the third cake's net gain, marginal revenue minus marginal cost.

Question 6 — Marginal cost · Application

Answer: (B)

$116 − $92 = $24.

  • (A) ✗ $116 is total cost at 6 cakes.
  • (B) ✓ Correct.
  • (C) ✗ $116 ÷ 6 is average total cost.
  • (D) ✗ $116 − $30 is variable cost at 6 cakes.
  • (E) ✗ $30 is the seventh cake's marginal cost.

Question 7 — Marginal cost · Analysis

Answer: (E)

The rent is a fixed cost: every total cost rises by $20, so every marginal cost is unchanged and the fifth cake is still the last whose marginal cost is below $21. Profit falls from $13 to $13 − $20 = −$7.

  • (A) ✗ The cost of one more cake has not changed.
  • (B) ✗ Baking more would add cakes whose marginal cost is above the price.
  • (C) ✗ The $20 is paid whatever the output, so it is not part of any cake's marginal cost.
  • (D) ✗ Right about output, wrong about profit, which falls by the whole $20.
  • (E) ✓ Correct.

Question 8 — The net gain of a unit · Application

Answer: (C)

The fourth cake brings in $21 and costs $74 − $60 = $14: its net gain is $21 − $14 = $7. Check: profit rises from $3 at 3 cakes to $10 at 4.

  • (A) ✗ $14 is the fourth cake's marginal cost.
  • (B) ✗ $10 is total profit at 4 cakes.
  • (C) ✓ Correct.
  • (D) ✗ Wrong sign: marginal revenue is above marginal cost.
  • (E) ✗ $3 is the fifth cake's gain, $21 − $18.

Question 9 — The net gain of a unit · Application

Answer: (A)

The gains are $9, $13, $11, $7 and $3, which add to $43. That equals profit at 5 cakes minus profit at zero: $13 − (−$30). The $30 rent is paid at every output, so no cake's marginal cost contains it.

  • (A) ✓ Correct.
  • (B) ✗ Right total, wrong reason: implicit costs are already in the total-cost table.
  • (C) ✗ The gains leave out the fixed cost, so they overstate the profit.
  • (D) ✗ $105 is total revenue at 5 cakes.
  • (E) ✗ Right total, wrong reason: the sixth cake is not among the five added.

Question 10 — The profit-maximizing rule · Analysis

Answer: (D)

With marginal revenue above marginal cost, one more unit adds to profit. Keep expanding until the two are equal.

  • (A) ✗ Cutting output gives up units that add to profit.
  • (B) ✗ Profit can still rise; the firm is short of the best quantity.
  • (C) ✗ The rule changes quantity, and does not compare price with marginal cost in general.
  • (D) ✓ Correct.
  • (E) ✗ Producing more, not nothing, is the response.

Question 11 — The profit-maximizing rule · Application

Answer: (E)

The fifth cake costs $92 − $74 = $18 and sells for $18. Profit is $72 − $74 = −$2 at 4 cakes and $90 − $92 = −$2 at 5. Where marginal revenue exactly equals marginal cost the extra unit changes nothing; the rule's quantity is 5.

  • (A) ✗ Profit at 4 cakes is −$2, not $2, and 5 cakes does just as well.
  • (B) ✗ The sixth cake costs $24 for $18 of revenue; profit at 6 is −$8.
  • (C) ✗ $90 is total revenue.
  • (D) ✗ The fifth cake leaves profit unchanged; it does not lower it.
  • (E) ✓ Correct.

Question 12 — The last unit worth producing · Application

Answer: (B)

At $27 the sixth cake costs $24 and the seventh $30. Bake 6: profit is $27 × 6 − $116 = $46.

  • (A) ✗ The seventh cake loses $3, so profit falls to $43.
  • (B) ✓ Correct.
  • (C) ✗ $43 at 5 cakes; the sixth cake adds $3.
  • (D) ✗ $162 is total revenue at 6 cakes.
  • (E) ✗ Profit at 8 cakes is $216 − $184 = $32.

Question 13 — The last unit worth producing · Synthesis

Answer: (C)

With whole units, produce the last unit whose marginal revenue is at least its marginal cost. The seventh cake adds $30 to cost and $27 to revenue, so baking it cuts profit from $46 to $43.

  • (A) ✗ Ben's error: the first unit with MC above MR is the first one not to produce.
  • (B) ✗ Profit is $46 at 6 cakes and $43 at 7.
  • (C) ✓ Correct.
  • (D) ✗ The sixth cake still adds $3 to profit.
  • (E) ✗ Right conclusion, wrong reason: profit per cake is higher at 5 ($8.60) than at 6 ($7.67).

Question 14 — The marginal revenue curve · Analysis

Answer: (A)

For a price taker each cake adds the market price to revenue, so the marginal revenue curve is a horizontal line at the price. A higher price moves the whole line up.

  • (A) ✓ Correct.
  • (B) ✗ It stays horizontal; the bakery still sells any number at the market price.
  • (C) ✗ Marginal revenue depends on the price, not on cost.
  • (D) ✗ Marginal revenue rises with the price.
  • (E) ✗ Marginal revenue is not drawn as a vertical line.

Question 15 — The marginal cost curve · Analysis

Answer: (D)

The first four cakes cost $12, $8, $10 and $14 more: marginal cost falls, then rises — the swoosh.

  • (A) ✗ The second cake costs less than the first.
  • (B) ✗ It rises from the third cake on.
  • (C) ✗ $12 is only the first cake's marginal cost.
  • (D) ✓ Correct.
  • (E) ✗ Average total cost at 2 cakes is $25, not $8.

Question 16 — The profit-maximizing quantity · Analysis

Answer: (B)

Between the two crossings marginal revenue is above marginal cost, so every unit from Q₁ to Q₂ adds to profit. Q₁ is a profit minimum; profit is largest at Q₂, where marginal cost crosses marginal revenue from below.

  • (A) ✗ Units just past Q₁ still add more revenue than cost.
  • (B) ✓ Correct.
  • (C) ✗ Units between the midpoint and Q₂ still add to profit.
  • (D) ✗ The lowest marginal cost is not where profit peaks.
  • (E) ✗ The two crossings give very different profits.

Question 17 — The profit-maximizing quantity · Application

Answer: (E)

At $15 the fourth cake costs $14 and the fifth $18. Bake 4: profit is $60 − $74 = −$14, the smallest loss available.

  • (A) ✗ The old price's answer.
  • (B) ✗ $10 was the profit at 4 cakes when the price was $21.
  • (C) ✗ Baking nothing loses the whole $30 rent, more than $14.
  • (D) ✗ The fourth cake adds $1; profit at 3 cakes is $45 − $60 = −$15.
  • (E) ✓ Correct.

Question 18 — Plotting marginal cost between quantities · Application

Answer: (C)

The sixth cake's $24 is the change from 5 cakes to 6, so it is plotted halfway, at 5.5.

  • (A) ✗ That is the convention for a total, not a change.
  • (B) ✗ Halfway, not at the lower quantity.
  • (C) ✓ Correct.
  • (D) ✗ $24 is the height of the point, not its quantity.
  • (E) ✗ 6.5 is where the seventh cake's marginal cost is plotted.

Question 19 — A price taker: marginal revenue equals price · Analysis

Answer: (A)

As one of many bakeries, Lakeside can sell any number of cakes at the market price, so each extra cake adds exactly the price to total revenue.

  • (A) ✓ Correct.
  • (B) ✗ A price taker does not set its price.
  • (C) ✗ Total revenue rises with every cake sold.
  • (D) ✗ With a downward-sloping demand curve, marginal revenue would be below the price.
  • (E) ✗ The bakery's profit has nothing to do with why its marginal revenue equals the price.

Question 20 — MR = MC for every firm · Application

Answer: (D)

Total revenue is $30, $56, $78, $96, $110, $120 and $126, so marginal revenue is $30, $26, $22, $18, $14, $10 and $6. The fourth trip's marginal revenue, $18, is above its $14 marginal cost; the fifth's, $14, is below its $16. Run 4 trips and charge the fare those 4 trips sell at, $24. Revenue at 4 trips is $96, and the four trips add $8 + $10 + $12 + $14 = $44 to cost.

  • (A) ✗ Where the fare is closest to marginal cost; the fifth and sixth trips each lose money.
  • (B) ✗ Right quantity, but $18 is the fourth trip's marginal revenue, not its fare.
  • (C) ✗ The fifth trip adds $14 of revenue for $16 of cost.
  • (D) ✓ Correct.
  • (E) ✗ Fare equals marginal cost between 6 and 7 trips, and every trip past 4 loses money.

Question 21 — MR = MC for every firm · Synthesis

Answer: (E)

The profit-maximizing rule compares marginal revenue with marginal cost for every firm. Price equals marginal revenue only when the firm can sell any quantity at the market price; a firm that must cut its price to sell more has marginal revenue below its price, as question 20 shows.

  • (A) ✗ True only for a price taker.
  • (B) ✗ Efficiency is not what a firm maximizes.
  • (C) ✗ Price equal to average total cost is zero economic profit, not maximum profit.
  • (D) ✗ Firms are assumed to maximize profit.
  • (E) ✓ Correct.

Question 22 — Whether to produce, then how much · Analysis

Answer: (B)

The rule finds the best quantity. Whether producing that quantity beats producing nothing — or leaving the industry — is a separate comparison.

  • (A) ✗ The rule finds the best output even when that output makes a loss.
  • (B) ✓ Correct.
  • (C) ✗ A price taker does not choose its price.
  • (D) ✗ Entry depends on economic profit in the long run.
  • (E) ✗ It answers only the second question.

Question 23 — Economic profit decides whether · Application

Answer: (C)

Accounting profit is $900 − $700 = $200. Economic profit subtracts the owner's forgone $250 as well: $200 − $250 = −$50. The owner's time would earn more elsewhere.

  • (A) ✗ Leaves out the implicit cost.
  • (B) ✗ The two figures are swapped.
  • (C) ✓ Correct.
  • (D) ✗ Adds the implicit cost instead of subtracting it.
  • (E) ✗ Right numbers, wrong decision: staying or leaving depends on economic profit.

Question 24 — Economic profit decides whether · Synthesis

Answer: (D)

The rent is owed whether the bakery bakes or not. At 4 cakes revenue is $60 and the costs that stop when it stops baking are $74 − $30 = $44, so baking covers them and $16 of the rent. Its loss is $14 against $30 if it closes. If the price stays at $15, it should leave when the lease ends.

  • (A) ✗ That is the long-run rule; in the short run the rent is owed anyway.
  • (B) ✗ The fourth cake's marginal cost, $14, is below the $15 price.
  • (C) ✗ The fifth cake costs $18 for $15 of revenue; the loss grows to $17.
  • (D) ✓ Correct.
  • (E) ✗ Nothing in the question tells us the accounting profit, and it would not decide this.

Question 25 — Profit maximization · Synthesis

Answer: (A)

Profit is $135 − $92 = $43 at 5 cakes and $162 − $116 = $46 at 6. Profit per cake is higher at 5 ($8.60 against $7.67), but the bakery maximizes total profit.

  • (A) ✓ Correct.
  • (B) ✗ The sixth cake earns less than the average but still adds $3.
  • (C) ✗ A rising marginal cost matters only once it exceeds the price.
  • (D) ✗ The seventh cake costs $30 for $27 of revenue; profit falls to $43.
  • (E) ✗ Profit at 8 cakes is $32; marginal revenue is constant but marginal cost is not.

Notes for the teacher

Content validity. All 16 concept entries appear. The rule and its two routes (entries 2 and 6 to 8) carry eight items; the goal and the two margins (entries 1 and 3 to 5) six; the graph (entries 9 to 12) five; who the rule applies to (entries 13 and 14) three; and the decision before the rule (entries 15 and 16) three. The weight follows the learning objective, which asks for the rule "using graphs or data" and puts the data first.

Bloom levels. Application 11 · Analysis 9 · Synthesis 5, a ratio of 1 : 0.8 : 0.5 against the standard's 1 : 3 : 1.75. The paper is more numerical than the standard because the module is a pair of tables, and the released papers test this topic from tables far more than from prose.

Questions 20 and 21 are the ones to discuss aloud. They are the generalisation the module never makes. Question 20's fare-equals-marginal-cost distractor is the commonest Unit 4 error, met here first.

Question 24 is Krugman's MCQ 4 corrected. The textbook keys "a firm should continue to produce as long as its economic profit is at least zero" (p. 540). That is the long-run rule; distractor 24A states it and is wrong for a bakery whose rent is owed until the end of the year. Topic 3.6 makes the short-run rule formal.

Distractor design. Every wrong option encodes a documented error. The recurring ones: total revenue reported as profit (2B, 2E, 11C, 12D); revenue maximization taken as the goal (3A, 3B, 25D, 25E); a total or an average read as a marginal (5A, 6A, 6C, 15E); the first unit with MC above MR produced (12A, 13A, 13B); fixed cost treated as part of marginal cost (7A, 7C, 9C); the lowest marginal cost taken as the target (3D, 16D); the falling-MC crossing chosen (16A, 16E); price equated with marginal cost for a firm whose marginal revenue falls (20A, 20E, 21A); and the long-run rule applied to a short-run decision (24A).

Three distractors are right-conclusion-wrong-reason, flagged in the solutions: 9B and 9E have the right total and the wrong explanation, and 13E picks Ana for a reason that is false at these numbers. 23E is the reverse — the right numbers and the wrong decision.
