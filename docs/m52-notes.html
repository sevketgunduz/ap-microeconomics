The headline. Module 52 is well aligned. Topic 3.4 has two essential knowledge statements, and the module delivers the core of both: explicit against implicit cost, accounting against economic profit, normal profit, and the rule that a firm should judge its business by economic profit. The numbers are clean, Babette's café is a good worked case, and the AP Review is usable almost whole. What it leaves out is the last third of CBA-2.C.2 and two of the learning objective's three verbs. The statement lists three implicit costs — "cost of financial capital, compensation for risk, or an entrepreneur's time" — and risk occurs 0 times in pp. 530–535; the phrase compensation for risk occurs 0 times in all 927 pages. Learning objective CBA-2.C(b) asks students to "explain how firms respond to profit opportunities", and enter, entry and exit are all 0 in the module; that is Module 60. And CBA-2.C(c), "calculate a firm's profit or loss", is examined on the released papers with average total cost — 109 hits — while Module 52 computes profit only from totals; the per-unit form is Module 59, p. 590. Krugman gives two definitions of accounting profit. The margin on p. 531 subtracts "the explicit cost and depreciation"; p. 539 says accounting profit uses "only the explicit costs". The exam follows the second: depreciation occurs 0 times in the Micro CED and 0 times on the eight papers. Teach depreciation as one of the explicit costs on the accountant's books.

Verdict: well aligned. Every term the framework names for this topic is taught here except one of three implicit costs, and the two learning-objective verbs the module does not reach are taught later in the same section of the book. The work in class is translation (Krugman's implicit cost of capital is the CED's cost of financial capital; his owner's time is the CED's entrepreneur's time) and three short supplements.

The essential knowledge statements this topic carries, verbatim:

CBA-2.C.1 (CED p. 65) — "Firms respond to economic profit (loss) rather than accounting profit."
CBA-2.C.2 (CED p. 65) — "Accounting profit fails to account for implicit costs (such as cost of financial capital, compensation for risk, or an entrepreneur's time), which, if fully compensated, result in normal profit."

The CED never says explicit cost. The two-word phrase occurs 0 times in the Micro CED — the framework defines accounting profit only by what it leaves out — but 11 times on the eight papers and 28 times in this module. Teach it as the exam does.

The CED's Sample Instructional Activity for this topic (activity 3, CED p. 61) is the module in miniature: "provide students with the salary and costs associated with a particular job and discuss whether that person makes accounting profit. Then discuss what implicit costs are and whether that person makes economic profit."

Krugman's three objectives (p. 530):

  1. The difference between explicit and implicit costs and their importance in decision making
  2. The different types of profit, including economic profit, accounting profit, and normal profit
  3. How to calculate profit

All three are CBA-2.C(a) and (c). Objective 3 is the calculate verb, and the AP Review practises it four times (CYU 2, MCQ 5, both free-response questions).

The margin definitions, in Krugman's own words

Explicit and implicit cost (p. 530) — "An explicit cost is a cost that involves actually laying out money. An implicit cost does not require an outlay of money; it is measured by the value, in dollar terms, of benefits that are forgone."
Accounting profit (p. 531) — "The accounting profit of a business is the business's total revenue minus the explicit cost and depreciation."
Economic profit (p. 532) — "The economic profit of a business is the business's total revenue minus the opportunity cost of its resources. It is usually less than the accounting profit."
Implicit cost of capital (p. 532) — "The implicit cost of capital is the opportunity cost of the capital used by a business—the income the owner could have realized from that capital if it had been used in its next best alternative way."
Normal profit (p. 534) — "An economic profit equal to zero is also known as a normal profit. It is an economic profit just high enough to keep a firm engaged in its current activity."

The two identities the whole topic reduces to

Accounting profit = total revenue − explicit costs Economic profit = total revenue − explicit costs − implicit costs = accounting profit − implicit costs

Released questions test the second line directly: 2015 multiple-choice question 35 ("Economic profit can be calculated as accounting profit minus which of the following?") and 2022 question 38 ("What is the difference between accounting profit and economic profit?") both key implicit costs.

5. Corrections to make explicitly in class

Accounting profit is revenue minus explicit costs — full stop

Krugman's margin on p. 531 subtracts "the explicit cost and depreciation"; two modules later, p. 539 says accounting profit uses "only the explicit costs". The exam's version is the second, and depreciation appears nowhere in the Micro CED or on the eight papers. The two are not in conflict once depreciation is understood as one of the costs an accountant records: tell students that, give them the short definition, and use the long one only when a Krugman item builds it in (MCQ 3, FRQ 2).

Economic profit is never more than accounting profit

The same margin says economic profit "is usually less than the accounting profit". It is always less or equal: implicit costs cannot be negative, so subtracting them can never raise the number. The two are equal only when implicit costs are zero. 2022 international question 32 offers "Accounting profits are less than economic profits" as a distractor, and 2024 question 32 keys "Accounting profit is greater than economic profit because accounting profit ignores implicit costs."

Normal profit is zero economic profit, not zero accounting profit

A firm earning normal profit has a positive accounting profit, exactly equal to its implicit costs. Krugman's FRQ 1 ($30,000 each) and CYU 2 ($25,000 each) both show it and the text never says it. The released papers test it repeatedly: 2016 question 24 offers "Its accounting profits exceed implicit costs" against a firm at zero economic profit, and the 2024 free-response question on Paolo's pineapple farm asks whether accounting profit is positive, negative or zero for a firm in long-run equilibrium. The answer is positive.

Zero economic profit is a good result

Krugman says it well (p. 534): a firm earning normal profit "could not do any better using its resources in any alternative activity". Students hear zero and think failure. Make them say the sentence aloud.

An implicit cost is a real cost

Students treat implicit costs as hypothetical because no money changes hands. Table 52.1 is the answer: the implicit cost of a year in college, $35,000, is larger than the explicit cost. Babette would be better off as a chef in someone else's restaurant, even though her books show $35,000 of profit.

FRQ 2(d): "operate if at least normal profit" is the long-run rule

Krugman's solution says Sunny should "operate if she makes at least normal profit". That is the rule for staying in a business. In the short run, with fixed costs already committed, a firm keeps producing at an economic loss as long as price covers average variable cost (PRD-2.A.1, CED p. 67; Topic 3.6). Sunny's stand has no fixed costs in the item, so the answer stands — but flag the distinction now, because Module 53's MCQ 4 states the same rule without a time frame.

6. What this module does not give you for Unit 3

  • How much to produce. Module 52 decides whether a business is worth running; the quantity

decision, marginal revenue against marginal cost, is Module 53.

  • Any cost curve. No fixed, variable, average or marginal cost — Modules 54–56. The per-unit form

of profit therefore waits for average total cost.

  • The market's response. Economic profit attracts entry and losses cause exit; the module says only

that profit "signals the best use of resources" (p. 533). The process is Module 60.

  • Risk. The third implicit cost in the framework's list is supplied in the guide.
